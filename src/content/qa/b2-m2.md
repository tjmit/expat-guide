Yes. The Migrant Helpline is 218 106 191 from a mobile or a foreign network, or 808 257 257 from a fixed line, on business days from 9:00 to 19:00. CNAIMs are support centres that bring several public services together in one place, so newcomers can deal with them in a single visit. The CNAIM page gives no addresses or phone numbers, so find your nearest centre through AIMA.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Helpline:** the gov.pt migrants page lists "218 106 191 (mobile and foreign network) or 808 257 257 (fixed network)", available on business days from 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. It says the line works in several languages and covers work, nationality, social security, housing, family and minors, and other issues. It does not say which languages. [UNVERIFIED] Whether English is available.
- **CNAIM:** the national centres bring several public bodies and specialist offices under one roof. The page names a reception and screening office, a social affairs and inclusion office and a legal support office. It also names immigration and border services, Social Security, Health, Education and a citizen space as bodies involved. It says the responsible entity is AIMA. The page warns "esta página pode estar desatualizada" (this page may be out of date) and points to the AIMA portal.
- **CLAIM (local centres):** the local-centre page could not be opened, so nothing is said here about them.
- **Not stated on any page read:** CNAIM addresses, opening hours, whether you need an appointment, and which services a US citizen on a work or teaching permit can use there. Ask the helpline.
