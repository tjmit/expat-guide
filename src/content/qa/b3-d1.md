It can. It depends on whether you remain domiciled in that state under its own rules, and those rules differ by state.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** No IRS source addresses state tax (Pub 54 does not). Indiana's Department of Revenue bulletin says a person leaving temporarily does not lose Indiana domicile unless they clearly show permanent intent to stay abroad, and a domiciled resident abroad is taxed on all income including foreign income. A Virginia Tax Commissioner ruling (one taxpayer's facts, not general law) says you must intend to abandon Virginia domicile with no intention of returning, a simple declaration is not enough, the burden of proof is on the person claiming the change, and registering and voting in Virginia is "very strong evidence" of domicile. Your own state's rule must be checked directly.
