You have three usual routes: a bank transfer, an online money-transfer service, or a cash wire service. Costs come from two places, a fee and the exchange rate you are given, so compare the total you receive in euros, not just the fee. Because the US is outside the European payment area (SEPA), expect to need more details than an IBAN. Fee figures below are one guide's numbers and are not confirmed.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Inside versus outside SEPA:** the guide says transfers inside SEPA usually need only the recipient's IBAN, while transfers outside it may need the recipient's name, account number, SWIFT/BIC, bank name and address and the reason for the transfer. The US is not a SEPA country. [UNVERIFIED] Check your US bank's own list of what it asks for.
- **Where the cost hides:** the guide says a bank may charge a fixed fee plus an exchange-rate markup, and that each institution sets its own rate. It gives "€20–30" as a typical retail-bank fee, and "1% to 6%" for cash wire services. It quotes an online service starting at "0.23%", but the article mostly promotes that service, so treat it as advertising, not a comparison. [UNVERIFIED] None of these figures was checked against a price list.
- **Timing:** the guide says SEPA transfers take one to two working days and others about five, and that Portuguese banks usually close their transfer desks at 15:00. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Official price check:** Banco de Portugal runs a Fees Comparator for payment-account fees, including credit transfers, showing the maximum price each Portuguese bank charges. The page read does not say whether it covers international transfers or exchange-rate margins, so ask your Portuguese bank for the total cost of a US-to-Portugal transfer in writing.
- **Not stated on any page read:** your rights if a transfer goes wrong, refund rules, and any limits or reporting thresholds. For US reporting of foreign accounts, see the Ongoing US obligations page.
