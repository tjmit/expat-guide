Pensions for past employment are taxable only in the State where you live. Social Security and public pensions may be taxed by the paying State, and that wording covers payments to a US citizen.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** Article 20(1)(a): pensions and similar remuneration for past employment "shall be taxable only in that State" (the recipient's residence State). Article 20(1)(b): social security benefits and other public pensions paid by a Contracting State to a resident of the other Contracting State "or a citizen of the United States may be taxed in the first-mentioned State" (the paying State). The wording is not exclusive. Government pensions are in Article 21(2). Protocol 1(c)(i) lists Article 20(1)(b) among the benefits the saving clause does not override. Not found in the text I read: any residence-State relief for Article 20(1)(b) payments, so whether Portugal also taxes US Social Security paid to a Portuguese resident is **REASONABLY TRUE / UNVERIFIED**. Earlier drafts said "both countries may tax Social Security under Article 20" citing a Senate report summary; that wording is withdrawn.
