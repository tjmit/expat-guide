Usually you need a residence permit valid for at least two years before family reunification, but the law exempts minor or dependent children and family of holders of art. 90 permits (teaching and highly qualified activity). Your family applies for a visa at a Portuguese consulate after AIMA authorisation.

## Citations and legal explanation

- Art. 98(1): the holder of a permit valid for at least two years has the right to reunification with family members covered by arts. 99 and 100. Art. 98(3): the two-year period does not apply to (a) minors or dependants, (b) a spouse or equivalent who is, with the holder, parent of such a minor or dependant, and (c) family members of holders of permits granted under arts. 90, 90-A or 121-A. Art. 98(4): a minister may waive or reduce the period in exceptional cases.
- **Discrepancy flagged:** the independent review states art. 90 holders are not on the exemption list. The consolidated text I read on Diário da República lists art. 90 in 98(3)(c). Resolve with a lawyer or AIMA before telling teachers they can bring family immediately.
- Art. 99: covered family: spouse; minor or incapable children; adopted minors; unmarried adult dependent children studying in Portugal; dependent first-degree ascendants; minor siblings under the holder's guardianship by a foreign decision. gov.pt's wording on siblings is broader than the law.
- gov.pt service page (updated 07.08.2026, may be out of date): apply at a consulate in the family member's country after AIMA authorisation.
