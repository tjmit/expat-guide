Some employers treat teacher pay as exempt under Article 22 of the US-Portugal treaty, which exempts teaching or research pay for up to 2 years from arrival. It applies only if every condition is met, and schools apply it in different ways.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** Conditions in the treaty text (G4): you were a resident of the other country immediately before visiting; you come at the invitation of the Government or of a university, accredited educational institution or recognized scientific research institution of Portugal (or under an official cultural exchange program); solely to teach or do research at it; for no more than 2 years from arrival; once only; not together with Article 23. After 2 years, or if conditions fail, pay is taxed under the general employment rule (Article 16). The Portuguese ruling (Processo 6762/2021, on a Portuguese researcher in the US) says the exempt income must still be declared in Portugal for rate purposes if you file, and that the person was exempt from filing when it was the only income. It does not say how an employer applies the article, and it does not say whether a private or international school counts as an "accredited educational institution". Points schools may read differently, all **REASONABLY TRUE / UNVERIFIED**: whether the school is an eligible institution; whether the staff member was a US resident immediately before arriving; how the 2-year clock is counted; and whether a long-term move is a "visit". Meanwhile the exemption never removes US tax for a US citizen (G3).
