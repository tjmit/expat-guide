Probably yes. The card is for short trips to other European countries, not for care in Portugal, and it is free. Portugal's Social Security (Segurança Social) guide lists people registered with Portuguese Social Security (workers, pensioners and their families) and legally resident users of the national health service (SNS), and it does not rule out foreign nationals. No page says outright that a US citizen with a residence permit qualifies, so ask Portuguese Social Security (Segurança Social) before you count on it. The card covers only necessary care at public hospitals and health centres. It is not travel insurance.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Covers:** necessary, urgent care during a temporary stay, on the same terms as that country's residents. You may still pay fees that are not refunded. The guide says it does not cover planned treatment, private care (unless the provider has a public agreement) or repatriation. It is not valid in the United States.
- **Countries:** the Portuguese Social Security guide lists the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland and the UK. The gov.pt pages leave out the UK, so check before you travel.
- **Who can ask:** the guide lists workers, pensioners and their family members, members of public or private health subsystems, and "utentes do SNS, residentes legais em Portugal". The gov.pt pages say you must live in Portugal and have a NISS (Portuguese Social Security number) and an SNS user number. The guide marks the SNS number "if applicable". No page states a nationality rule. [UNVERIFIED] Whether a US citizen falls into a listed group is not stated. The 13 November 2026 guide says a non-EU national registered with Portuguese Social Security under a passport should replace it with a valid residence permit.
- **How:** free, through Segurança Social Direta, at a Portuguese Social Security counter or a Loja do Cidadão, before you travel. The guide says it arrives in 5 to 7 working days and is valid 3 years, or the length of the residence permit.
