Mail the FBI report to the US State Department Office of Authentications with form DS-4194, a $20 fee and a prepaid return envelope, naming Portugal as the destination. Plan for 4 to 12 weeks by mail. Photocopies are rejected. A private company can do it for you, from about $57 to $200 or more before shipping. That is a convenience, not an endorsement.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Federal process:** State Dept: "You can send FBI and police records to the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications to get an apostille." The "Requesting Authentication Services" page (last updated May 15, 2023) says "Authentications fees are $20 per document for all services," "You must request authentication services by mail," and "We will process your request in 12 weeks from the date we receive it." The "About the Office of Authentications" page says "We will process your request in 4 weeks" (undated).
- **Avoiding rejection:** "All seals and signatures must be originals." Name the destination country on the request so you get an apostille, not an authentication certificate. Pages list rejection causes such as copies, missing or wrong notarization, and wrong certification order. Use trackable mail, include a prepaid return envelope, and watch the 90-day window for the FBI report.
- **State documents** (rules differ by state): the order is issued to you, then notarized if required, then clerk of court certification if applicable, then the Secretary of State apostille. New York charges "$10 per document" and offers same-day walk-in service in New York City and Albany.
- **Company options, as published on their own sites:** APVI $149 service fee plus $20 State Dept fee, "10 Business Days." US Legalization $57 (3 to 5 weeks) or $77 (8 business days) per FBI report. Southeast Spanish $100 to $200 depending on speed, and its page contradicts itself on turnaround. Whether State fees and shipping are included varies. Their turnaround claims were not tested, and the State Dept page says its own mail-in time is 12 weeks, so ask any service how it gets faster handling. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Not retrieved:** independent reviews, refund policies, and whether you mail the original FBI envelope to the company.
- **Our inference, not sourced:** do not open a sealed FBI envelope before the apostille unless the apostille service or State Dept tells you to. [UNVERIFIED]
