**In plain words:** the US-Portugal tax treaty has a rule, Article 22, for teachers and researchers who come from one country to the other for a limited time. If you qualify, the pay for that work is not taxed by either country for up to 2 years.

**You qualify only if all of these are true:**

1. You were a resident of the US right before you came to Portugal.
2. You came at the invitation of the Portuguese government, or of a university, accredited educational institution or recognized research institution in Portugal.
3. You came only to teach or do research there.
4. It is within 2 years of the day you arrived.
5. You have not used this benefit before. It works once only.
6. You are not also using Article 23 of the treaty.

**Three things to know:**

- It does not stop the US taxing you as a US citizen. See G3.
- After 2 years, or if any condition is not met, your pay is taxed as ordinary employment income.
- We found nothing that settles whether a private or international school counts as an accredited educational institution, or how an employer should apply the rule. Ask your HR department and a Portuguese tax professional. See J2 and J3.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Treaty text, Article 22(1), verbatim fragments:** "An individual who is a resident of a Contracting State immediately before visiting the other Contracting State", visiting "at the invitation of the Government of the other Contracting State or of a university or other accredited educational institution or recognized scientific research institution of that other Contracting State" (or under an official cultural exchange program), "solely for the purpose of teaching or carrying out research at such a university or educational institution", "for a period not exceeding 2 years from the date of his arrival in the other State", "shall be exempt from tax in both Contracting States on his remuneration from such activity". The benefit applies only once and never together with Article 23. Article 22(2) excludes research undertaken primarily for the private benefit of specific persons.
- **After the exemption:** pay is taxed under the general employment rule (Article 16).
- **Saving clause:** because of the saving clause (G3), the exemption does not shield a US citizen from US tax.
- **Portuguese ruling (Processo 6762/2021, on a Portuguese researcher in the US):** it lists the same conditions. It says exempt income must still be declared in Portugal for rate purposes if you file, and that the person was exempt from filing when it was the only income. It does not say how an employer applies the article, and it does not say whether a private or international school counts as an "accredited educational institution".
- **Points schools may read differently, all REASONABLY TRUE / UNVERIFIED:** whether the school is an eligible institution; whether the staff member was a US resident immediately before arriving; how the 2-year clock is counted; and whether a long-term move is a "visit".
- **Related answers:** G4 (the treaty wording), G3 (saving clause), J1 (do I file in Portugal), J2 and J3 (what schools do and whether an employer can decide).
