Mostly no. The saving clause lets the US tax its citizens as if the treaty had not come into effect, except for a short list of articles.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** Protocol 1(b): notwithstanding the Convention, "the United States may tax its citizens, as if the Convention had not come into effect" (including some former citizens for 10 years if tax avoidance was a principal purpose of losing citizenship). Protocol 1(c)(i) keeps for all US citizens and residents: Article 9(2) associated enterprises, Articles 20(1)(b) and 20(4), and Articles 25 (relief from double taxation), 26 (non-discrimination) and 27 (mutual agreement). Protocol 1(c)(ii) keeps Articles 21 (government service), 22 (teachers and researchers), 23 (students and trainees) and 29 (diplomats) only for individuals who are "neither citizens of, nor have immigrant status in" the US. So a US citizen cannot use the Article 22 exemption against US tax.
