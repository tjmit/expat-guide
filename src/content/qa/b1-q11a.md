An apostille certifies the seal and signature on a public document so Hague Convention countries such as Portugal accept it. The US State Department apostilles federal documents like the FBI report. The issuing state's Secretary of State handles state documents such as birth and marriage certificates. For the visa, only the FBI Identity History Summary is definite. Teaching credentials, diplomas, family civil records and translations are not confirmed by the VFS pages, so ask your VFS center or consulate in writing for the final list.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Who issues it:** USAGov: "Documents such as vital records issued by a U.S. state will need an apostille from that state's secretary of state" and "Federal documents will need an apostille from the U.S. Department of State." State Dept: "The U.S. Department of State will not issue an apostille for state-issued documents."
- **Definite: FBI Identity History Summary** (federal document, State Dept apostille); see the FBI check answer.
- **Possible: teaching credentials, diplomas, transcripts.** The older VFS one-pager asks for proof you are "enabled to practise" a regulated profession or have "high professional qualifications"; it does not say apostilles are required. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Possible: marriage certificate and children's birth certificates** for accompanying family. Family checklists were not researched. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Possible: state or local police records, court, divorce or name-change papers, powers of attorney.** The checklist names the FBI for US residents. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Translations:** foreign-language documents need a certified English translation notarized as a true translation for the US apostille process. Portuguese translation rules were not researched. [UNVERIFIED]
