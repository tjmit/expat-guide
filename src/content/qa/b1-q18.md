No official source was found. Banks and carriers set their own rules, so call both before you leave. One US rule matters once you open a Portuguese bank account: the IRS FBAR.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** IRS FBAR page (last reviewed or updated July 30, 2026): a US person must file an FBAR if "the aggregate value of those foreign financial accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time during the calendar year reported"; "an account at a financial institution located outside the United States is a foreign financial account"; the report is "due April 15 following the calendar year reported." A Portuguese account would be a foreign account; a US account held while you live abroad is not defined as one on that page (the IRS does not address that case directly). Whether US banks may close accounts of customers with a foreign address, how FDIC insurance applies, and carrier policies for keeping a US number abroad: no official source retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED]** The D3 checklist asks for US address proof (lease or driver's license) at the time of application, so keep US documents current until then.
