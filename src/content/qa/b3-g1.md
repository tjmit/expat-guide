Yes. Signed at Washington on September 6, 1994, in force from December 18, 1995, and effective for taxes from January 1, 1996.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** Official title: Convention for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with Respect to Taxes on Income, with a Protocol. Cover note: "Entered into force December 18, 1995." Article 30(2): it enters into force on exchange of ratification instruments, effective for taxes withheld at source on amounts paid on or after the following 1 January and for other taxes for taxable years beginning on or after the following 1 January. Article 31: either country can end it after 5 years with 6 months' notice. Residence tie-breaker (Article 4(2)): permanent home available; if both, closer personal and economic relations (centre of vital interests); then habitual abode; then nationality; then mutual agreement. Article 16(1): salaries and wages are taxable only in the residence State unless the employment is exercised in the other State; 16(2) removes source-State tax only if you are there 183 days or less in a 12-month period, the employer is not a resident of that State, and the pay is not borne by a permanent establishment there.
