Yes for ordinary items, with restrictions; not for prescription medicine. Expect to sign for the parcel: if nobody is there to receive it, it can be returned.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Reader experience (the site author, not an official source):** Shipments will probably need a signature on delivery. If you are not there when it arrives, it can be sent back. One box of personal items bounced between Boston and Lisbon three to four times before it was finally returned to the sender (a relative's address in the US) and nearly lost. Whether a signature is required for a given service, and the redelivery and return rules, were not found on the official pages read. **[UNVERIFIED]** Tips from this experience: arrange for someone to be at the address, use a service with tracking and delivery-attempt notices, and confirm the return address is one you can reach.
**Details (USPS International Mail Manual, page undated):** prohibited items include arms and weapons, butane lighters, valuables such as jewelry, coins and banknotes, live animals and plants, tobacco. Import permits are needed for shipments Portugal regards as commercial. Personal shipments valued at $300 or more need a duplicate invoice. Priority Mail International 66 lb limit; First-Class Package International 4 lb limit. "Global Express Guaranteed service is suspended as of September 29, 2024." "The VAT exemption on imported goods was abolished as of July 1, 2021." The State Dept Portugal page says Portuguese law prohibits mailing prescription medicines and such parcels "will be impounded."
