Probably yes, if your plan allows it. Unlike the IRA rule, the plan rules appear to count excluded foreign earned income as compensation. Whether it saves you any tax is a separate question.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** IRS retirement-plan training text (quoting Treas. Reg. 1.415-2(d)(2)(i)): compensation "also includes foreign earned income without regard to the exclusions under IRC sections 911, 931 and 933." Pub 571 (Rev. 1-2026), verbatim: 403(b) includible compensation includes "Income otherwise excluded under the foreign earned income exclusion." A private letter ruling (PLR 202302007, non-precedential) allowed Roth deferrals from wages regardless of a section 911 election. 2026 limits (Notice 2025-67): elective deferral $24,500; annual additions $72,000; age-50 catch-up $8,000. Not stated anywhere I read: whether pre-tax deferrals from fully excluded pay produce any tax benefit, and whether a given plan accepts deferrals from a person abroad (plan document decides).
