Yes. Portugal runs two programmes for adult newcomers. Português Língua de Acolhimento (PLA) is described as free and certified, and the IEFP Português para Todos (PPT) programme is open to immigrants aged 18 or over with regularised status in Portugal. Public schools, employment centres and Qualifica Centres run them. Cost and whether a US citizen on a work or teaching permit qualifies are not confirmed, so ask before you enrol.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **PPT (IEFP):** the page says it is for "cidadãos imigrantes, com idade igual ou superior a 18 anos, empregados ou desempregados, com situação regularizada em Portugal" (immigrants aged 18 or over, employed or unemployed, with regularised status). It lists courses at A2 and B2 level of 150 hours each, and a 25-hour technical Portuguese certificate. It says there is no minimum schooling requirement and points to an online pre-registration. [UNVERIFIED] Whether a residence permit counts as "regularised status": the page never mentions permits or nationality.
- **PLA:** the UNHCR page says "you can apply for a free, certified language training programme called Português Língua de Acolhimento (PLA)", run by public schools, IEFP centres (the public employment and vocational training centres) and Qualifica Centers, and says to contact AIMA's Portuguese language promotion office to enrol. This page is a refugee-help site, not a Portuguese government page.
- **Not stated on any page read:** fees, start dates, waiting lists, how many hours a week, and whether the certificate is accepted for any visa or citizenship purpose. For the citizenship requirement, see the question on Portuguese level in the Renewals and citizenship page.
- **Overlap:** the First 90 days page has a short answer on where to learn Portuguese. This one adds the course details.
