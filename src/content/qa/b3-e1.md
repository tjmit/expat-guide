Yes, effective August 1, 1989. It prevents paying both countries' social security taxes on the same work and lets credits from both countries be combined for partial benefits.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** It covers old-age, survivors and disability insurance. Agreement text: employment is generally under the laws of the country where it is performed (Art. 4(1)); posted workers stay under the sending country's system if the posting is not expected to exceed five years (Art. 5(1)(a)); self-employed people are covered by their country of residence (Art. 4(2)). To combine credits, SSA says you need at least six US credits (about 1.5 years) and Portugal requires at least 12 months of Portuguese coverage. Each country pays its own benefit. Pub 54 adds that US social security and Medicare taxes generally do not apply to wages for services performed as an employee outside the US except in listed exceptions. (The pamphlet's Portuguese retirement-age figure is dated 2019 and is not repeated here.)
