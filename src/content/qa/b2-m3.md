The three big networks are MEO, Vodafone and NOS, and smaller brands run on them. A prepaid SIM needs the least paperwork. A monthly contract usually asks for your NIF, so you may need your tax number first. Ask the operator exactly what it wants before you go to the shop.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Operators:** the guide names MEO, Vodafone and NOS, plus virtual operators on those networks. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Contract paperwork:** the guide lists valid ID, proof of address, NIF and bank account details, and says you "won't be able to sign up for a Portuguese mobile contract without your NIF". [UNVERIFIED]
- **Prepaid:** the guide says you typically give your name and a valid ID, and that top-ups may need a Portuguese bank account. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Minimum terms:** the guide says mobile contracts "typically run for a 24-month loyalty period" and that no-commitment contracts cost more. The official gov.pt pages say only that a minimum term may apply and that cancelling early may mean a penalty or returning equipment. See the existing answer on cancelling an internet or phone contract. [UNVERIFIED] The 24-month figure.
- **Keeping your number:** the guide says many operators let you keep an existing number, but gives no process. For keeping a US number, see the existing answer on phone numbers and bank access.
- **Not stated on any page read:** the legal basis for SIM registration, whether a NIF is needed for prepaid, and number portability steps. Verify with an operator or ANACOM before relying on any of this.
