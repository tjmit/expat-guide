A resident of one country who visits the other at the invitation of its government, university, accredited educational institution or recognized research institution solely to teach or do research is exempt in both countries for up to 2 years from arrival, once only.

## Citations and legal explanation

**Details:** Article 22(1), verbatim fragments: "An individual who is a resident of a Contracting State immediately before visiting the other Contracting State", visiting "at the invitation of the Government of the other Contracting State or of a university or other accredited educational institution or recognized scientific research institution of that other Contracting State" (or under an official cultural exchange program), "solely for the purpose of teaching or carrying out research at such a university or educational institution", "for a period not exceeding 2 years from the date of his arrival in the other State", "shall be exempt from tax in both Contracting States on his remuneration from such activity". Benefits only once; never together with Article 23. Article 22(2): not for research undertaken primarily for the private benefit of specific persons. The Senate letter of submittal paraphrases it as exempt "in the other country"; the treaty text says "both Contracting States". Because of the saving clause (G3), the exemption does not shield a US citizen from US tax. A Portuguese binding ruling (Processo 6762/2021) applied the same article to a Portuguese researcher in the US and listed the same conditions.
