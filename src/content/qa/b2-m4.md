You can choose any operator. The offer, including any minimum term, goes in your contract, so read it before you sign. Promotional offers often come with a minimum term, and leaving early may mean paying a penalty or returning equipment. ANACOM, the communications regulator, has a tariff comparison tool.

## Citations and legal explanation

- **Free choice:** both pages say the market is open and "you can choose any market operator." No law sets minimum service quality or a maximum installation time, so those must be written in your contract.
- **Minimum terms:** the English page says you "may have to agree to a minimum term for that service" for a promotional deal, and that cancelling during it may mean a penalty or returning equipment. The length is not stated. [UNVERIFIED] Any figure such as 24 months comes from commercial sources.
- **Compare first:** both pages point to ANACOM's tariff comparison tool (comparador de tarifários) at anacom.pt. [UNVERIFIED] The tool itself was not opened.
- **Moving into a home that already has service:** the English page says you are not obliged to keep the previous occupant's operator. If you want a new one, the page says you must cancel the previous contract before switching operator.
- **No coverage:** if the service "cannot be provided due to lack of cover", the page says you can cancel and ask for reimbursement.
- **Problems:** see the existing answer on cancelling or complaining about an internet or phone contract.
